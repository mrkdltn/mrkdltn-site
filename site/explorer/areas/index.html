Report card areas in context

Ofsted's report cards grade each school in up to eight areas. This page sets those grades beside what we know about secondary schools' intakes and results, to see which areas track context most closely. It describes patterns across England; it says nothing about whether any single grade is right.

Same results, different grades

Each chart shows the share of inspected secondaries graded below Expected standard in one area, across five groups of schools. By default the groups are Ofsted's IDACI quintiles, based on how deprived the areas pupils live in are. Switch to group them instead by the disadvantaged share of each school's GCSE year group, split into fifths across every secondary with results; the two measures agree closely but not exactly. The solid line keeps only schools whose 2024/25 GCSE results were close to or above their 50 similar schools in the open replica, so the comparison holds results roughly level. If grades followed results alone, the solid line would be close to flat.

Other parts of context

Deprivation is not the only thing that differs between schools. These charts take three areas and the part of context most closely tied to each: how many pupils joined at the usual time, the prior attainment of the year group and the share with an education, health and care plan. Fifths are drawn across every secondary with published results.

Context, held constant

The model results are loading.

Each row is one report card area. The open circle is the gap in the chance of a below Expected grade between schools in the most and least deprived IDACI fifths, with deprivation alone. The filled circle is the same gap with other context held constant: for primaries, SEN, size, region, urban or rural setting and governance; for secondaries, also prior attainment, pupil stability and 2024/25 results against 50 similar schools. Lines show 95% confidence intervals. A gap is read as clear only if it is at least 5 points and its interval excludes zero, a rule set before any result was seen.

Phase

How these estimates were made

  • The analysis was planned in writing before any grade was set against any context: the pre-registered plan was committed on 25 November 2026 at 16:00 UTC (commit 801f11b). The results file reports every pre-registered estimate, including the nulls, and the robustness checks.
  • Each area is modelled with a logistic regression of being graded below Expected standard (Needs attention or Urgent improvement). The gap is the average marginal effect of being in the most rather than the least deprived IDACI fifth, with robust (HC1) standard errors. Areas with fewer than 10 schools below Expected standard per model term use a reduced model; where even that is short, the estimate is drawn in grey and not read. Ethnicity, trust and inspector identifiers are never used.
  • The models are re-run each week on the latest public releases by open code; the numbers behind these charts record each source, its release and its checksum.

    Limits

    • Unobserved factors: inspection judges much that no public data set records, and anything left out of the models may sit behind the gaps.
    • Deprivation, pupil mobility, prior attainment and SEN overlap, so holding some constant also removes part of what deprivation describes. In secondaries, prior attainment accounts for most of the fall from the raw to the controlled gap.
    • Selection into inspection: the weights adjust for what is observed about who has been inspected, not for anything else in Ofsted’s scheduling.
    • Primary context is thin: there is no public school-level measure of primary prior attainment, mobility or results.
    • Secondary results are 2024/25 gaps against an open replica of the similar-schools model, not the official list.
    • Ofsted’s data holds only each school’s latest full inspection.

    Reading this carefully

    • These are associations in a non-random set of inspections, not evidence of bias. That holds for the models too: holding some context constant does not show that any grade is wrong. Deprivation, mobility, prior attainment and need overlap heavily, and inspection judges more than any one of them.
    • The first year of report cards is not a random sample of schools: who was inspected first depends on Ofsted's scheduling.
    • Results are one year of provisional data matched to an open replica of the similar-schools model, not the official list. "Close to or above" uses the 2024/25 results inspectors would have seen.
    • Groups of fewer than 10 schools are not plotted. On a computer, hover over a point for its exact share and the number of schools behind it.
    • Sources: Ofsted management information on state-funded school inspections (report cards), updated daily from reports.ofsted.gov.uk; DfE key stage 4 performance data, 2024/25 and 2025/26 (provisional). Method and code are open.
