    How to read this

    Raw results are not the school's effectAttainment 8 tracks intake closely. The national comparison mostly tells you about the intake.
    Similar schools ask a fairer questionHow did schools with a comparable intake do? It is a comparison, not a judgement, and Ofsted says it does not decide grades.
    One year is noisyA cohort of 150 pupils can move several points from year to year for reasons nobody controls. Look for patterns over years.
    This is a replicaThe official groups use pupil-level data that is not public. Treat these neighbours as a close approximation and check the IDSR.

    How the Explorer works

    Every comparison on this page, how it is made and where the data comes from. The figures below use the school you are looking at.

    Each secondary is compared with the 50 schools whose intakes are most like its own, following the DfE and Ofsted similar-schools model (technical note, 16 July 2026). The official groups use pupil-level data that is not public, so this is an open replica built from published figures.

    What each factor counts for

    The published weights. POLAR4 is not public, so the replica leaves it out and shares its weight among the rest in the same proportions.

    How this school’s 50 are chosen

    The data behind each factor

    Prior attainment
    Key stage 2 profile of the 2023/24 GCSE year group, carried from a predecessor school where the URN changed; estimated for 34 schools.
    Disadvantaged pupils and pupil stability
    Shares of the 2025/26 GCSE year group.
    Area deprivation
    Ofsted’s IDACI quintile, from pupils’ home postcodes.
    SEN support, EHC plans, English as an additional language, pupil numbers
    January 2026 school census and SEN statistics.

    Each factor is put on one scale (standard deviations from the England average), weighted, and the distance from this school to every other secondary is measured. The 50 nearest form the group, and their average result is the comparator.

    One model, fitted across all 3,298 secondaries with 2025/26 results, of what schools with a given intake typically achieve. It uses the same factors as the similar-schools model, in the spirit of the DfE’s contextual value added measure of 2006 to 2010.

    How this school’s expected result is built

    Each bar is one factor’s push on the expectation, largest first, starting from a school at the England average on every factor. It follows the results measure chosen above.

    Attainment 8
    A straight-line regression that explains about three quarters of the variation between schools.
    English and maths, grade 5 or above
    Fitted to the share of pupils (a fractional logit), so expected values always stay between 0% and 100%.

    Each set of results is read against four comparators. The words in the Reading column follow fixed rules, set out here.

    ComparatorHow the reading is decided
    National averageAbove or below the national figure for 2025/26.
    50 similar schoolsAbove or below only when the gap is larger than chance would explain: 1.96 standard errors, combining pupil-level spread (14.2 Attainment 8 points, from 2023/24 Progress 8 confidence intervals) with the spread within the group. “Well above” or “well below” also needs a gap larger than one standard deviation of the group. Otherwise “close to”.
    Contextual modelIn line within ±2 Attainment 8 points, or ±4 percentage points for English and maths, for a median-sized GCSE cohort of 180. The band scales with the square root of cohort size: wider for smaller cohorts, narrower for larger ones.
    Progress 8, 2023/24The DfE’s own published bands, which combine the score with its 95% confidence interval. The last published year, carried from a predecessor school where the URN changed; the DfE did not publish Progress 8 for 2024/25 or 2025/26.

    Likely ranges. Each gap is shown with a likely range: the gap plus or minus a margin for chance. For the similar schools, the contextual model and Progress 8 it is the same margin the reading uses, so a range that crosses zero goes with “close to”, “in line” or “average”; the national reading is simply above or below. For the national average and the similar schools it is 1.96 standard errors, from the pupil-level spread of Attainment 8 (14.2 points, from 2023/24 Progress 8 confidence intervals) or, for English and maths, the binomial spread at the school’s own percentage, with the spread within the group added for the similar schools. For the contextual model it is the cohort-scaled band above. For Progress 8 it is the DfE’s published 95% confidence interval. The range describes chance variation from one year’s pupils; it does not account for errors in the model or the data.

    Last year. For Attainment 8, the similar-schools row also gives the 2024/25 reading and gap: the school’s 2024/25 final result against its 50 similar schools’ 2024/25 results. The other comparisons are not available for 2024/25, and English and maths 5+ is shown for 2025/26 only.

    Readings use their own colours, green above, grey close and rust below, and never Ofsted’s grade colours, which are kept for inspection grades only.

    Terms

    Attainment 8
    A pupil’s average grade across eight GCSE-level slots, with English and maths double-weighted; the school figure is the average across pupils.
    English and maths 5+
    The share of pupils achieving grade 5 or above in both English and maths.
    Disadvantaged
    Pupils eligible for free school meals at any point in the last six years, looked after, or adopted from care.
    IDACI
    Income Deprivation Affecting Children Index: the share of children in an area living in income-deprived families.
    IDSR
    Ofsted’s inspection data summary report, which lists a school’s official 50 similar schools.
