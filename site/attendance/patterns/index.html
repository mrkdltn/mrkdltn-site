How this page works

Every chart on this page, how it is made and where the figures come from.

These charts show what goes with absence across schools in England, and how that has changed since 2013/14. They show association, not cause.

ChartHow it is made
The gap by free school mealsPrimary and secondary schools are put in five equal groups by the share of pupils eligible for free school meals. Each group is taken together for every full school year. Schools are grouped by their newest census figure and keep that group in every year, so each line follows the same schools back in time.
School by schoolEach dot is one school in the autumn and spring terms 2025/26, placed by its absence and by one of its circumstances from the January 2026 census. The line joins the middle school of each twentieth of schools along the axis.
How schools are spreadIn each full school year the schools are put in order of overall absence. The line is the middle school. The bands mark the middle half of schools and the middle eight in ten. The edges of the bands are estimated from counts of schools in narrow bands of absence (0.25 percentage points wide for primary schools, 0.5 for secondary and 1 for special), so each edge is an estimate to within a band of that width.
Region by regionToday’s schools in each region are taken together for the newest full school year and set against England. The change is the difference, in percentage points, between 2018/19 and that year, set against England’s change.
One square a dayEach square is one school day in England, from the in-year figures. It is coloured against the average day of the last full school year. A day is left blank where fewer than 80% of the usual sessions were due.
Severe absenceThe share of pupils who missed half or more of their sessions in each full school year, for England’s schools of each phase taken together.
Overall absence
The share of possible sessions that pupils missed, whether authorised or not. A session is a morning or an afternoon.
Persistent absence
The share of pupils who missed a tenth or more of their possible sessions.
Severe absence
The share of pupils who missed half or more of their possible sessions.
Taken together
One rate for a group of schools, so that larger schools count for more. It is not the average of the schools’ own rates.
Points
A difference between two rates is given in percentage points, shortened to “points”.
The middle school
The school with as many schools above it as below it: the median.

Association, not cause. The charts show what goes with absence across schools. They do not show that any circumstance causes it. They describe schools, not pupils, and a pattern across schools need not hold for the children within them.

Everything on this page is built from school-level files published by the Department for Education under the Open Government Licence v3.0.

  • Department for Education, Pupil absence in schools in England, school-level files: full school years; autumn and spring terms.
  • Department for Education, Schools, pupils and their characteristics, school-level file for January 2026 (published June 2026).
  • Department for Education, Special educational needs in England, school-level file for January 2026 (published June 2026).
  • Department for Education, Pupil attendance in schools, the in-year figures from the registers schools share each day, for the day-by-day calendar only. These are official statistics and differ from the termly census statistics used everywhere else on this page.
Which years
The charts over time use full school years, 2013/14 to 2024/25. No figures were published for 2019/20, so every chart leaves that year empty. The school-by-school chart and the match for similar schools use the autumn and spring terms 2025/26.
Which schools
The charts follow today’s schools back in time. Where a school has exactly one predecessor its earlier years are included.
Circumstances
Free school meals, SEN support, education, health and care plans and first language are each school’s figures from the January 2026 census.

School pages set each primary and secondary school beside the 20 schools most like it. The match uses the factors in this table and nothing else.

Special schools are not matched, so there is no table for them. Choose primary or secondary above to see the factors.

One scale
Each factor is standardised, so a difference of the same size counts the same whichever factor it is on.
Weights
Each factor is weighted by how strongly it goes with overall absence across schools of the same phase. The weights come from a regression of overall absence on these factors. The regression is used only to set the weights.
The 20 nearest
The distance from a school to every other school of its phase is measured across the weighted factors. The 20 nearest are its similar schools.

No expected rate is calculated for any school. Region is left out. Ethnicity is never used. Special schools are not matched.

The page gives rates for schools and for groups of schools. It never gives a count of pupils.

RuleWhat it means here
Fewer than 10 pupils on rollThe school is left out of every chart.
Rates onlyAbsence is always a percentage. Where a number of schools is given, it is a count of schools, never of pupils.
Schools, not pupilsEvery figure describes a school or a group of schools. Nothing here describes a group of pupils within a school.
Severe absenceShown for England by phase only. It is never shown for a single school.

These charts are a reading of published figures. They are not a judgement on any school.

  • No cause. A circumstance that goes with higher absence across schools has not been shown to produce it.
  • No pupils. A pattern across schools need not hold for the children within them.
  • No expected rate. Nothing on this page, or on any school’s page, says what a school’s absence ought to be.
  • No named schools. The dots are not labelled and no school is ranked.
  • No region in the match, and no ethnicity anywhere.
  • No similar schools for special schools, and no free school meals groups for them.
  • No persistent absence in the chart of how schools are spread, which uses overall absence only.
  • No figures for 2019/20, which were not published.
