    Sources and method

    Source. Department for Education, Pupil absence in schools in England: . Accredited official statistics from the school census. School details from Get Information about Schools. Open Government Licence v3.0.

    Measures. Overall absence is the share of possible sessions missed. Persistent absence is the share of pupils who missed 10% or more of their own possible sessions. Figures cover pupils aged 5 to 15.

    Two kinds of period. The newest figures cover the autumn and spring terms only and are compared with the same two terms a year earlier. The long run uses full school years. The two are never mixed on one chart.

    England and the local authority. These are pooled across schools of the same phase in the DfE’s school-level file: sessions missed over sessions possible, and persistent absentees over enrolments.

    Similar schools. For primary and secondary schools, the 20 nearest schools of the same phase on eligibility for free school meals, first language other than English, SEN support, education, health and care plans, size, rural setting and, for secondaries, selective admissions. Each factor is weighted by how strongly it relates to absence. This is our own match from published data, not the DfE’s. Context figures are from the January 2026 school census.

    No expected rate. The Explorer does not calculate what a school’s absence “should” be. It sets each school beside real schools in similar circumstances and leaves the reading to you.

    Special schools. No similar schools are given: what a special school provides for shapes its attendance far more than the context measured here.

    Trusts. The date a school joined its present trust is from Get Information about Schools and is marked on the year-by-year chart. Where the DfE recorded a different trust for the school before that date, it is named; those records begin in 2020/21.

    Schools that changed. Where a school has exactly one predecessor, for example before becoming an academy, the earlier years come from the predecessor and the chart marks the join. Schools formed by merging others start at the merger.

    What is not shown. Rates only, never counts of pupils. No figures for a school with fewer than 10 enrolments. Severe absence (missing half or more of sessions) is not shown for single schools because it is usually a handful of children. The DfE publishes no breakdown by pupil group for individual schools, so none appears here.

    Small schools. In a school of 100 pupils one child is a full percentage point of persistent absence. Read small differences accordingly.

    Ofsted. The attendance and behaviour grade is taken from the school’s most recent report card and is shown as published, with its date.
