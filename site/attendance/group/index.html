Sources and method

Source. Department for Education, Pupil absence in schools in England, school-level files: . Trust and constituency of each school from Get Information about Schools. Open Government Licence v3.0. The DfE publishes no absence figures for trusts or constituencies; these are built from its school figures.

Three measures. Overall absence is the share of sessions missed. Persistent absence is the share of pupils who missed a tenth or more of their sessions. Severe absence is the share who missed half or more. Severe absence is given for areas, trusts and pupil groups, never for a single school, and not where fewer than 10 pupils are severely absent.

Pooled rates. Sessions missed across the schools over sessions possible, and persistent and severe absentees over pupils on roll. Phases are kept apart because primary, secondary and special schools differ so much that a single figure would mostly describe the mix.

When a pooled rate is shown. Only where at least three schools of the phase contribute, and for a trust only where it has five or more schools in all. Smaller groups list their schools without a pooled figure.

Trusts over time. The solid line on a trust’s chart pools only the schools that had joined it by the end of December of each school year, so it is the trust’s own record. The dashed line pools every school it holds today in every year, including years before they joined and a predecessor school where there was exactly one. Joining dates are from Get Information about Schools. Where the DfE recorded a different trust for a school before its joining date, the school is shown as having come from that trust; the DfE’s records of this begin in 2020/21, so earlier transfers are not identified, and a trust that only changed its name is not counted as a transfer. The list includes the trusts of some maintained foundation schools, which have no joining dates.

Constituencies. 2024 boundaries. A figure describes the schools located in the constituency, not the children who live there; many pupils cross boundaries to school.

Local authorities. The 153 authorities responsible for schools. A local authority’s figures here are pooled from its primary, secondary and special schools in the DfE’s school-level file, so they can differ slightly from the DfE’s own authority totals.

Pupil groups. Absence for pupils eligible for free school meals, with SEN support, with an education, health and care plan, and with no identified SEN, from the DfE’s “Absence by pupil characteristics” for the autumn and spring terms (published 15 October 2026). The DfE publishes these for local authorities, regions and England only, so they appear on local authority and county pages and not for single schools, trusts, constituencies or wards. County figures pool their authorities. No figure is shown for a group of fewer than 10 pupils.

Change. “Change in a year” sets the autumn and spring terms of 2025/26 against the same two terms of 2024/25. “Change since 2018/19” sets the full year 2024/25 against 2018/19, the last full year before the pandemic; for pupil groups, which are published for the two terms only, it sets the autumn and spring terms of 2025/26 against those of 2018/19. Places are coloured against England’s change, so green means absence fell, or rose less than it did across England. For trusts, change describes the schools held today.

Wards. May 2026 wards, with boundaries from the Office for National Statistics (Open Government Licence v3.0; contains OS data © Crown copyright and database right 2026). Only primary schools are pooled, and only where a ward has three or more: secondary pupils travel too far for a ward figure to mean much, and about 44% of wards with a school carry a figure. As with constituencies, a figure describes the schools in the ward, not its children.

Counties. The 48 ceremonial counties, each made up of whole local authorities. Stockton-on-Tees, which straddles two, is counted with County Durham. Local authority figures on county pages are pooled from schools and so differ slightly from the DfE’s own authority figures, which include other settings.

The funnel. Each trust’s rate is set against its number of pupils. The curved bands show where about 95% and 99.8% of trusts of a given size fall, widened to match the spread actually seen between trusts. Small trusts vary more by chance alone, so position within the band says little.
