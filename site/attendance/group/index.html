How this page works

Three measures. Overall absence is the share of possible sessions that pupils missed. A session is a morning or an afternoon. Persistent absence is the share of pupils who missed a tenth or more of their sessions. Severe absence is the share who missed half or more.

Taken together. Each figure for a place is one rate for its schools taken together: sessions missed across the schools over sessions possible, and persistently and severely absent pupils over pupils on roll. Larger schools count for more, so it is not the average of the schools’ own rates. Primary, secondary and special schools are kept apart, because they differ so much that a single figure would mostly describe the mix.

Points. A difference between two rates is given in percentage points, shortened to “points”. A rate of 7.0% against 6.5% is 0.5 points higher.

Change. “Change in a year” sets the autumn and spring terms of 2025/26 against the same two terms a year earlier. “Change since 2018/19” sets the full year 2024/25 against 2018/19, the last full year before the pandemic. Pupil groups are published for the two terms only, so for them it sets the autumn and spring terms of 2025/26 against those of 2018/19. For trusts, change describes the schools held today.

Colours. Green marks lower absence than England and rust higher. Where a change is shown, green means absence fell, or rose less than it did across England, and rust means it rose more. Colour shows direction only. A named school is never coloured on these pages.

Trusts over time. The solid line on a trust’s chart takes together only the schools that had joined it by the end of December of each school year. It is the trust’s own record. The dashed line takes together every school the trust holds today in every year, including years before they joined and a predecessor school where there was exactly one. Joining dates are from Get Information about Schools. Where the DfE recorded a different trust for a school before its joining date, the school is shown as having come from that trust. The DfE’s records of this begin in 2020/21, so earlier transfers are not identified. A trust that only changed its name is not counted as a transfer. The list includes the trusts of some maintained foundation schools, which have no joining dates.

The funnel. Each trust’s rate is set against its number of pupils. The band shows where 95% of the trusts on the chart fall, allowing for size. It is drawn from the trusts themselves, not from a theory of chance, and it is wider for small trusts because their figures move more from year to year. Being outside the band says a trust is unusual for its size. It says nothing about why.

Pupil groups. Absence for pupils eligible for free school meals, pupils not eligible, pupils with SEN support, pupils with an education, health and care plan and pupils with no identified SEN. The figures are from the DfE’s “Absence by pupil characteristics” for the autumn and spring terms (published 15 October 2026). The DfE publishes them for local authorities, regions and England only. They appear on local authority and county pages, and not for single schools, trusts, constituencies or wards. A county’s figures take its local authorities together. No figure is shown for a group of fewer than 10 pupils.

Local authorities. The 153 local authorities responsible for schools. A local authority’s figures here take together its primary, secondary and special schools in the DfE’s school-level file. They can differ slightly from the DfE’s own totals for the authority, which include other settings.

Counties. The 48 ceremonial counties, each made up of whole local authorities. Stockton-on-Tees, which straddles two, is counted with County Durham.

Constituencies. Parliamentary constituencies on their 2024 boundaries. A figure describes the schools located in the constituency, not the children who live there. Many pupils cross a boundary to go to school.

Wards. Wards as at May 2026. Only primary schools are counted, and only where a ward has three or more. Secondary pupils travel too far for a ward figure to mean much. Fewer than half of wards with a school carry a figure. As with constituencies, a figure describes the schools in the ward, not the children who live there.

Trusts. A trust’s schools are those recorded for it in Get Information about Schools. The DfE publishes no absence figures for trusts or constituencies. The figures here are built from its figures for their schools.

Small numbers. A figure for a place is given only where at least three of its schools of the phase contribute, and for a trust only where it has five or more schools in all. Smaller places list their schools without one. No school with fewer than 10 pupils on roll is shown. Rates only, never counts of pupils.

Severe absence. Severe absence is given for areas, trusts and pupil groups. It is never shown for a single school, where it usually describes a handful of children, and it is withheld where fewer than 10 pupils are severely absent.

No expected rate. No expected rate is calculated for any school. Each place is set beside England and beside others of its kind, and the reading is left to you.

Not a judgement. Every figure here is a reading of published data, not a judgement on a school, a trust or an area. Schools are listed in alphabetical order and never ranked. Ethnicity is never used.
