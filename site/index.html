About

Leading schools.

I run a secondary school in the West Midlands, and I write about what national education policy looks like from inside one.

I have led my current school, which serves one of England's most deprived communities, since 2023, through a period of substantial improvement that is the work I am proudest of. Across my career I have led schools in both regional and national trusts, mostly in communities where disadvantage shapes the daily work, and that has taught me how much of a school's improvement depends on the system around it: how it is held to account, how it is supported, and how well it grows its own teachers.

Those are the problems I care most about. Accountability should be fair: measures ought to reward what matters, and context should sharpen judgement rather than lower expectations. Disadvantage should not decide a child's outcomes, and a real sense of belonging is part of how it stops doing so. And schools need more good teachers than the system currently trains and keeps.

I'm always glad to hear from school and trust leaders, researchers, policymakers and journalists working on the same questions.
