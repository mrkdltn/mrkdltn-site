## 2. Words against data

**Results sentences:** achievement sentences that mention published results (outcomes, tests, GCSEs, the phonics check and so on). "Pupils achieve well" on its own does not count. About one sentence in five qualifies, and 91% of sections have at least one.

**Direction of each results sentence:**

- **Positive:** above, higher than, improved, achieve highly.
- **Negative:** below, declined, fewer, "not as strong as", "do not achieve as well as".
- **Level:** in line with, close to, broadly similar.
- **Mixed:** both positive and negative in one sentence.
- **None:** mentions results but says nothing about them.

"Not" and similar words within three words, in the same clause, flip a cue. So "not as strong" is negative, and "not below" is level.

**Four choices for you:**

1. Add **"level"** as its own direction. It is 15% of results sentences, and the plan has no place for it. This needs a one-line plan amendment.
2. Leave **"none"** sentences out of each section's overall reading, so one neutral sentence does not make a section "mixed".
3. Count **trend** ("improved", "declined") as direction, and report a version without it as exploratory.
4. Score "**results understate the school**" ("current work is stronger than results suggest") as negative about results.

**One honest limit:** achievement text is written to the grade, so reading it blind to the grade is only partly possible.
